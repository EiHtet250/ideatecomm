**MINT Museum of Toys**

Visitor Knowledge Base for the MINT Adventure Guide Chatbot

*Compiled on 30 September 2026 from the museum's own website and public ticketing listings.*

**1. How the assistant should use this document**

This document is the reference source for the MINT Adventure Guide assistant. The assistant should answer visitor questions from the content below and should not add facts from general knowledge. Every section is written so that it can be understood on its own.

Information carries one of three reliability levels, which are explained fully in Section 14. Facts taken from the museum's own website are official. Facts reported only by ticketing partners or visitor reviews are described in the text as reported. Topics that the museum has not published are listed as not confirmed. When a visitor asks about a reported or unconfirmed item, the assistant should give the information it has, state plainly how certain it is, and offer the museum's contact details from Section 12.

The assistant should answer in short, plain sentences and give the most useful point first. It should offer more detail only if the visitor asks, so that children, older visitors and readers of English as an additional language can follow easily. It should never invent opening times, prices, exhibit facts, directions inside the building or the location of the visitor. For emergencies, and for any request that needs a person to help in the building, the assistant should direct the visitor to a member of staff or to the Help page of the MINT Adventure Guide.

**Choosing a route**

The museum's website presents the levels in rising order, beginning with Collectables on Level 2 and finishing on the Rooftop. A visitor has reported that reception staff advised starting on Level 5 and working downwards at one's own pace. Either direction is acceptable. Visitors who use the stairs will pass the stairwell exhibitions, which are easy to miss when using the lift.

The following suggestions come from this guide and are not an official itinerary. For a short visit of about 45 minutes, a visitor could see Outerspace and Characters, which are the most recognisable themes for children. For a visit of about one hour, a visitor could add Childhood Favourites. For a full visit of about two hours, a visitor could see all four levels, the stairwell exhibitions and the Rooftop, and use the augmented reality touchpoints along the way. Visitors interested in history and social stories should allow extra time on Level 2.

**6. Exhibition guide by level**

Object locations use the row and shelf numbers shown on the museum's labels. Dates are as given by the museum, and the abbreviation c. means around that date.

**6.1 Level 2: Collectables**

This level treats vintage toys as historical artefacts. The visitor is guided through several themes. Early Toys and Traditional Folk Games recalls the kampong era, when children made games from household items, and features marbles, spinning tops, kites and board games. The Industrial Revolution section follows toy materials from wood and celluloid to lithographed tin, die cast metal and plastic. Globalisation and Commercialisation explains how toys were copied across borders and became merchandise, using the climbing monkey toy and Mickey Mouse products as examples. History and Politics shows how toys recorded events such as the Second World War, the changing years of modern China and the coronation of Queen Elizabeth II. Social and Cultural toys examine refugee experiences in Hong Kong through hand sewn dolls and racial segregation and the early Civil Rights Movement in the United States.

Content notice. Part of this level displays historical toys that contain racial stereotypes. The museum states that it shows them to raise awareness of discrimination and to start discussion, and it acknowledges that some visitors may feel discomfort. The assistant should mention this if a visitor asks about the level or is planning a visit with young children.

**6.2 Level 3: Childhood Favourites**

This level connects toys with childhood memories and with learning through play. Teddy Bears from Around the World presents makers from Germany, the United Kingdom and China, including Steiff and Merrythought bears and bears from the Shanghai Doll Factory. Dolls from Around the World includes the Door of Hope dolls, made from 1901 by a mission in Shanghai in which girls learned sewing and earned wages for dressing wooden dolls in detailed traditional costumes. Puppetry features Punch and Judy, German Kasperle Theatre, characters from the Chinese novel Journey to the West and Bruce Lee marionettes. Highlights mentioned by the museum include a Steiff teddy bear about 120 years old, the puppet Mr Punch and a doll known as the smallest doll in the world.

**6.5 Rooftop: The Public Gallery and Vintage Enamel Signs**

The Rooftop is called The Public Gallery. It is a gallery and event space that welcomes collaborations with artists and curators. It displays vintage enamel signs, which appeared in the mid 1800s as advertisements for food, household goods, fuel and services in the United Kingdom. Because they were made of coloured glass fused to iron plates and were displayed outdoors, they were known as street jewellery. They had largely disappeared by the 1950s. The collection is organised into Tasks at Home, Infant Care, Delicacies of the Middle Class, Industrial Alehouses, and Travel and Transportation.

**6.6 Stairwell exhibitions**

Each stairwell landing holds a small themed display, and reports state that each has an augmented reality touchpoint on the wall. Vintage Decorative Tins shows ornate biscuit tins of the late 1800s, such as the Literature tin by Huntley and Palmers. Birth of Astro Boy presents toys of the Japanese robot hero created by Osamu Tezuka, including an Astro Boy riding a tricycle and the Astro Boy Rocket. Spooky Horror collects monster and horror toys, including the Nutty Mad Car. Bedtime Stories gathers toys from fables, nursery rhymes and classic children's novels. Beatles Memorabilia includes records, merchandise and toys such as the Corgi Yellow Submarine issued in 1968.

**7. Notable objects**

The museum highlights several rare items. It holds the only Mekon Mask from the Dan Dare comic that is known to exist. It holds a teddy bear by Steiff that is about a century old. It also holds a doll from Alice's Adventures in Wonderland that belonged to Alice Liddell, the girl who inspired the character. Other pieces that visitors often ask about are the Lone Star Aqua Jet on Level 5, the Chinese Stairs on Level 2 and the Door of Hope dolls on Level 3.

Some travel listings mention a rare Bandai Batman figure and a Popeye and Olive Oyl tank toy from Japan. These are reported by third party sources, and the current display location of each has not been confirmed. The assistant should not tell a visitor that they are on a particular level.

**9. Accessibility and inclusive visiting**

The museum presents its collection in layers. A visitor can read the short theme introduction on each level, look at individual object labels that give the maker, date, material and origin, follow the stories in the augmented reality app, or join a guided tour for more depth. This allows each visitor to choose their own pace and level of detail.

Wheelchair users. Several ticketing partners state that the museum is wheelchair accessible, and one lists the experience as wheelchair accessible. A visitor reports that there is a lift, seating on every floor and toilets on each floor. The museum has not published step free entrance details, so a visitor who needs specific arrangements should contact the museum before visiting.

Visitors of short stature, children and people who use seating. Visitors report that the display cabinets are tall and that items on the highest shelves are hard to see. One partner notes that some information panels are placed high. Corridors on each level are narrow, with cabinets along one side. Seating is reported on the floors, and visitors may rest at their own pace.

Families with young children. Children aged six and under enter free. The museum offers a colouring space, board games, an augmented reality experience and school holiday activities. Children should be supervised, and the toys must not be touched.

Older visitors. Seniors aged 60 and above have a separate ticket category. The building has multiple levels, so the lift and the seating reported on each floor are useful, and visitors can view one or two levels rather than all of them.

Visitors with low vision, hearing loss or sensory needs. The museum has not published information about audio description, braille labels, sign language, hearing loops, quiet hours or sensory friendly arrangements. The Virtual Museum offers spoken narration, and guided tours give spoken explanations. The assistant should not claim that any other accommodation exists and should offer to help the visitor contact the museum by WhatsApp or email.

Service animals. Registered service animals are reported to be allowed, while other pets are not.

Language. This guide can answer in the languages it is set up to support. The museum's labels and programmes are described on its website in English, and the guided tours are described as being conducted in English. Other language support inside the museum is not confirmed.

**11. History, building and organisation**

The museum began as the idea of its founding chief executive, Mr Chang Yang Fa, a toy collector who is passionate about design and the arts. He wanted a space where visitors of all backgrounds could rediscover the past, rekindle childhood memories and reimagine the future. When it was founded in 2007 it was the first purpose built toy museum in the world. The MINT Shop and a dining space opened with it, and in 2015 the dining space became Mr Punch Public House. The name comes from the museum's collection of Punch and Judy puppets.

The building was designed by SCDA Architects to house the collection. Its facade has no external lighting that could damage the exhibits, and it is made of twenty six glass panels shaped like a wave. The building won or placed in international architecture awards, including runner up for Commercial Building at the Cityscape Dubai Architectural Review Awards in 2006, Best New Global Design at the Chicago Athenaeum International Architectural Awards in 2007, and the Tourism, Travel and Transport category at the Cityscape Dubai Architectural Awards in 2008.

The museum also displays a mural called Krusedull by the Norwegian cartoonist and designer Einstein Kristiansen, an abstract interpretation of the collections on Outerspace, Characters, Childhood Favourites, Collectables and the stairwell exhibitions. The museum serves locals, tourists, families, collectors, artists and students, and describes its mission as a journey of rediscovery that rekindles a moment of imagination and nostalgia with toys.

**13. Frequently asked questions**

**What is MINT?**

MINT stands for Moment of Imagination and Nostalgia with Toys. It is a private museum in Singapore that displays vintage toys and collectables, and it describes itself as the largest vintage toy museum in Asia.

**Where is the museum?**

It is at 26 Seah Street, Singapore 188382, near Raffles Hotel. The nearest MRT stations are Bugis and Esplanade, each about five minutes on foot.

**When is the museum open?**

It is open from Tuesday to Sunday, 9:30 am to 6:30 pm, and closed on Mondays. Last admission is at 5:30 pm.

**How much is a ticket?**

General admission for an adult starts from S\$30. A partner listing shows S\$21 for children aged 7 to 12 and for seniors, and free entry for children aged 6 and under. Please confirm current prices at ticketing.emint.com.

**Do I need to book in advance?**

Tickets can be bought online with instant booking, which is convenient, and guided tours must be booked before the visit. Whether walk in tickets are always available has not been confirmed.

**How long should I stay?**

Most visitors take one to two hours. A guided tour takes 60 minutes and the augmented reality experience adds extra time.

**Which floor should I start on?**

Either direction works. The website presents Level 2 first and finishes on the Rooftop, while a visitor was advised by reception to start on Level 5 and go down.

**What is on each level?**

Level 2 is Collectables, Level 3 is Childhood Favourites, Level 4 is Characters and Level 5 is Outerspace. The Rooftop has the enamel sign gallery, and small exhibitions are on the stairwell landings.

**What is best for young children?**

Characters on Level 4 and Outerspace on Level 5 are the most familiar to children. The colouring space, board games and augmented reality experience also suit them. Children aged six and under enter free.

**Is the museum suitable for wheelchair users?**

Ticketing partners describe it as wheelchair accessible, and a visitor reports a lift and seating on the floors. For specific needs, please contact the museum on WhatsApp at +65 8339 8966 before visiting.

**Can I take photographs?**

Photography is reported to be allowed in most areas, but flash and tripods are not permitted.

**Can I bring food, drink, a large bag or a pet?**

Outside food and drink and large bags or suitcases are reported as not allowed. Pets are not allowed except registered service animals.

**Is there a guided tour?**

Yes. The Around the World in 60 Minutes tour starts from S\$33 and must be booked in advance. A free 15 minute tour is offered with some ticket bundles, depending on availability.

**What is the augmented reality experience?**

It is a story led experience in the MINT Museum of Toys app with more than 37 touchpoints. Visitors scan markers near the toys to see animations that explain the toys' history.

**Can I explore the museum online?**

Yes. The Virtual Museum is a 360 degree tour with narration, animated clips and mini games, available through emint.com.

**Is there a shop, cafe or bar?**

There is a MINT Shop, reported on Level 1, and an online shop. The museum also has dining and bar space in the Basement and on the Rooftop, but the current opening times are not confirmed.

**Are there toilets and seating?**

A visitor reports toilets on each floor and seating on the floors. This has not been confirmed by the museum.

**Why does Level 2 show offensive looking toys?**

The museum shows some historical toys that contain racial stereotypes in order to raise awareness of discrimination and encourage discussion. It acknowledges that some visitors may feel uncomfortable.

**What is the rarest item in the museum?**

The museum highlights the only known Mekon Mask from Dan Dare, a Steiff teddy bear about a century old, a doll that belonged to Alice Liddell, and the Lone Star Aqua Jet, which it describes as the only known piece.

**Who founded the museum?**

Mr Chang Yang Fa, a toy collector, founded it in 2007. He remains the chief executive.

**What are the Door of Hope dolls?**

They are wooden dolls made from 1901 by a mission in Shanghai, where girls sewed and embroidered the costumes and were paid for their work. They are displayed on Level 3.

**Can I hold an event or wedding there?**

Yes. The Rooftop, Levels 2 to 5 and the Basement can be booked for weddings, pop up events and corporate functions. Enquiries are made through the museum's event enquiry form on emint.com.

**What should I do in an emergency or if I need help?**

Alert a member of staff immediately. In a life threatening situation call 995 for an ambulance or fire service, or 999 for the police. For non urgent help use the Help page of the MINT Adventure Guide.

**How do I contact the museum?**

Use WhatsApp or call +65 8339 8966, or email info@emint.com.
